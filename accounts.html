How accounts work

Aquasafe and PoolCheck: your account, your saved tests, and who can see them

An account keeps a copy of every test you save, and only you and your organisation's admins can see it.

Create an account inside Aquasafe or PoolCheck with a username and a password; one account works in both apps. From then on, each time you tap Save, the test is kept on your phone as before and also sent to your account. Without an account the apps work exactly as they always did, and tests stay on the phone only.

No internet? Nothing is lost. The test waits on the phone, the bar at the top says how many are waiting, and they upload the next time the app is open with a connection. The reading goes first and the photo second, so a slow 2G link still delivers the number. A test that has reached your account shows ☁︎ in the history.

Who sees what is enforced by the database, not by the app.

Every request carries your identity, and rules inside the database decide what comes back: your own tests, never another collector's. Changing the app or calling the server directly does not get around this. Your organisation's admins see every collector's tests, photos and accounts on a separate admin page that needs a password and a 6-digit code from an authenticator app.

Your photo is kept exactly as your phone saved it. A fingerprint of the photo is recorded when you save; when an admin opens it, the page checks the fingerprint still matches.

The choices behind it, and what they mean for you

WhatWhy, and what it means for you
Username and password, no emailMany field staff do not use email. If you forget your password, ask your admin to set a temporary one; there is no email reset.
Anyone can create an accountYou can start on day one. A new account sees only its own tests; admins can switch off accounts that should not be there.
Tests are never deletedA wrong test (wrong site, practice photo) is marked void by an admin with a reason, so the record shows the correction.
OTO is never shown as a passOTO measures total chlorine, so it cannot show that free chlorine is enough. A zero OTO still means zero free chlorine.
Tests saved before you signed inThey upload only if you tap “Upload earlier tests”, because a shared phone's old tests may not be yours. They upload without photos.
Shared phonesSign out before handing the phone over. Tests not yet uploaded stay on the phone and upload when their owner signs in again. “Tested by” follows whoever is signed in.
Switched-off accountsIf an admin switches your account off, the app tells you, and new tests stay on the phone until it is switched back on.
Fair-use limitsUp to 500 tests a day per account, so one account cannot fill the shared free storage. Normal use is far below this.

Your data

What is stored: your username and name, and for each saved test the reading, time, GPS location and the stamped photo. Who sees it: you and your organisation's admins. How long: tests 3 years, photos 12 months. Data is held in Supabase's Mumbai (India) region and backed up weekly. To have your account and name erased, ask your admin; the anonymous test numbers are kept as the organisation's water-safety record.

Limits worth knowing

These are screening tools, not certified laboratory results. An app installed to the home screen keeps its saved tests most reliably; on iPhone, a site not on the home screen can lose its stored data after 7 days unused, which can cost a photo that had not uploaded yet (the reading itself is kept in the test log). To print an address and map on the photo, the apps look up your location with OpenStreetMap when online.
